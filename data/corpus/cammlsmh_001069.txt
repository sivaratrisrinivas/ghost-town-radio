GENERAL MAHONE. 





As this distinguished Virginian 
is just now occupying a large por- 
tion of the public thought of the 
people of the United States, by 
reason of his being regarded as the 
“balance of power’? in the next 
Senate, the following clever pen 
portrait of him taken from a 
Washington City journal, will be 
read with interest: ‘‘Geaeral 
Mahone, of Virgina, or ‘Mahone, 
Virginia,’ as he registers himself 
at hotels, is a very small man. 
He will probably sink the beam at 
say ninety odd pounds and in 
height measures 5 feet 6 inches. 
The biggest thing about him is bis 
hat. It is aslouch and worn in 
typical Southern style. His hair, 
iron gray, is worn rather long, but 
does not hang way down over the 
neck. He has a beard and mus- 
tache, also iron gray. These hir- 
sutes, while not deserving to be 
called ‘straggling,’ are not thick 
in growth. His right hand is con- 
stantly feeling around his beard 
while he talks. Hestands straight 
and dresses well. He wears black 
broadcloth or a black coat and 
gray pants, His vest is unbottoned 
all the way down to the last two 
holes. This shows a length of per- 
fectly white linen. The collar is 
equally white, but not stiff, and is 
surrounded by a narrow black tie 
carelessly knotted infront. Around 
his neck is hung a long gold chain 
which winds in and out the button 
holes of the vest and finally disap- 
pears in the watch-pocket. Then 
there are his feet. They are very 
small. They are.perhaps the nar- 
rowest belonging to any man in 
this country. They would drive a 
Chicago girl mad with envy. Gen- 
eral Mahone is evidently rather 
proud of his feet. They are en- 
cased in small calf skin shoes, 
with low quarters. He wears car- 
dinal red stockings, and when he 
sits and talks with you one foot 
goes to the other knee, and the leg 
of the pantaloons being drawn up 
by this operation you get a good 
idea of that upon which he stands. 
Heisa ready talker and has the 
Southern accent pretty strongly 
marked. His voice is weak, that 
organ being comparatively as small 
as his feet, but is rather musical. 
It is evident that he is a thinker 
and is a pretty big man for such a 
small body. He will be the Alex. 
Stephens of the Senate.’

California Rifle Association Match. 

SAN Francisco, Nov. 21.—The 
California Rifle Association tom- 
pleted the competition of their fall 
meeting to-day at Shell Mound 
Park, Oakland. The weather was 
favorable, and the shooting good. 
The military short range match 
was won by Nick Williams, by a 
score of 47 out of a possible 50. 
Military mid-range was won by J. 
E. Klein, by a score of 48. Gov- 
ernor’s trophy by J. H. Robertson, 
score 47. Carey trophy by Nick 
Williams, score 47. Pacific Life 
challenge cup by H. C. Smith, 
score 24 out of a possible 25. Sub- 
scription match by J. EH, Klein by 
a full score, 25.

Fire and Loss of Life: 

San Francisco, Noy. 21.— 
Shortly before 3 o’clock this morn- 
ing the residence of John Campbell, 
on Kansas, between Humboldt 
and Nevada streets, and Potrero 
ayenue, was entirely destroyed by 
fire. Mrs. Campbell and eight 
children were rescued, but Mr. 
Campbell was foand in the ruins. 
He was anexpressman, and kept 
his horse in the basement of the 
house. He was last seen going 
home with liquor, andit is sup- 
posed that while attending to the 
horse he set the hay on fire. He 
was a native of Ireland, aged 46 
years. 

The Infperial Mine on Fire. 

VIRGINIA, Noy. 22.—The Con. 
Imperial is on fire on the 2050 foot 
level. The fire caught from a 
sconce between the time of the 
change of shifts this morning. No 
men arein the workings at this 
point. Five men who were below 
have been taken out. The men 
are bulkheading to stop the fire. 
At one time it was under partial 
control, but got under way again.

PILGRIMS’ PROGRESS, 





Or the Turns, Vrials and Tribula- 
tions of the Holiand Exploring 
Expedition. 


EpitTor STANDARD-NEwSs:— 
“Thus far into the bowels of the land 
have we journeyed withoutimpediment.” 

Verily your correspondent has 
fallen among the Philistines— 
Philistines who number in their 
ranks a most formidable corps of 
howling and uncongenial infants ; 
Philistines whose standard is for- 
ward ignorance, and whose motto 
is uncleanliness. Let us review 
the outfit: Holland (who by the 
way isnot one of the tribe afore- 
mentioned) it is almost needless to 
describe—it is the same Pioche, 
Bodie, California Pat. Holland, 
plus afew alterations in attire. A 
patent stage-robber cap adorns his 
massive head, while the collar of 
his overcoat, assisted by a downey 
scarf, protects a throat that knows 
full well the exhiletating worth of 
Bodie soda. His faultless feet are 
encased in boots of the latest fash- 
ion and a pair of government pants 
but poorly conceals the graceful 
symetry of his powerful limbs, 
He is a bird—a bird entirely. 

Elster needs but a word, there 
has been no change in him save a 


quiet smile that shows him to be 
wrapt in the sublime thoughts of 
his vast responsibility. 

Milliken! I find myself ata loss 
to describe Milliken. He is our 
purveyor, our eyes turn eagerly 
upon him, and we love him. To 
him are we indebted for our dainty 
meals, to him do we look for our 
camp fire amusement and (in our 
eyes at least) heis beautiul. He 
soars with Holland—he is another 
be bird.”’ 

Thorndike, well he’s my pard. 
I might expatiate on his insipient 
beard and rugged overalls, but I 
won't, Suffice it to say he looks 
* tough,” 

Now for the Philistines : Head- 
ing the list is John Hayseed Bow- 
man. Biblical investigations have 
bowed his back, and Lord only 
knows what has rusted the springs 
in his knees. A thin growth of 
stubble, cut off square at the lips, 
covers the lower portion of his an- 
gular features; and it is a profound 
problem to find his eyes. He is no 
“bird.” 

Then comes our; ‘‘family’’ this 
consists of one husband, in 
‘‘biled shirt’’ and standing collar, 
two ravishingly beautiful wives, 
whose Beatrician feet scorn those 
plebian articles—stockings, and 
eight (there or thereabouts) bawl- 
ing youngsters. Add to this 
three teamsters, and for their de- 
scription find some other teamster. 

About 7:30 A.M. on the 12th 
inst., our sixteen mules braced to 
the work and off we started on our 
pilgrimage. The air was frosty 
and biting, and as our luck would 
have it there was no warming and 
nutritious element around to brace 
up the pilgrims. Holland had not 
started with us: The proverbial 
rocky road to Dublin isn’ta cir- 
cumstance to the one we traveled. 
It was a case of thump, bump and 
jerk if we rode, and slip, stumble 
and cuss if we walked. More than 
this we were freezing. ‘Towards 
evening, however, we pulled up at 
the Goat Ranch and began prepar- 
ations for supper. You, no doubt, 
at sometime during your life have 
dropped some water among a lot 
of oats, and watched them spread 
out over the ground in wild con- 
fusion; well, just so did those child- 
ren branch out from their parent 
stems, If a man went to chop 
down some sagebrush he was 
more than aptto brain a future 
p-esident; if he rushed off to relieve 
the coffee pot from iis horizontal 
position the chances were ten to 
cne of his trampling down some 
precocious offspring. If any hot 
water was spilled it struck a baby, 
and more than once some one of 
the little darlings had to be rescued 
from the camp kettle or the frying 
pan. 

How we cooked that meal with- 
out roasting something human 
must ever remain a mystery. But 
we did, and undergoing the de- 
lightful sensation of toasting on 
one side and freezing on the other, 
we ate it. 

Hardly had we quaffed: the last 
draught of cold and muddy coffee 
whenathrilling war whoop startled 
us from our elysian happiness and 
announced the arrival of Comman- 
der Elster, Major-General Pat. Hol- 
Jand and Col. Milliken. How we 
welcomed them! how we ‘‘froze”’ 
to that subtile liquid that they not 
only brought in them, but with 
them! And how they regaled us 
with graphic accounts of the joy 
and delight the citizens evinced 
upon their exit from Bodie. How 
vividly did Holland portray the 
universal hilarity, the harmonious 
strains of silver tongued music, 
the immense concourse of exhiler- 
ated inhabitants, and his final sep- 
aration from his best and dearest 
friends. It was grand, and at the 
same time it was pathetic. Ere he 
had finished those hardy teamsters 
could be seen brushing away the 
silent tears that trickled down their 
brawny cheeks—the sage brush 
smoke may have assisted Pat. but 
at any rate those teamsters wept. 
AS soon as our emotions were some- 
what allayed, thoughts of repose 
came upon us. It was then that 
Holland’s chivalry declared stself. 
Not forty yards from where we sat 
was a house, clustering around his 
benevolent knees was the phalanx 
of infants, shivering and snufilng 
in the cold. The idea suggested it- 
self at once that these little pets 
should be enioying the comforts of 
that hearth. 

Execution rapidly — followed. 
Milliken was dispatched to inter- 
view thé owner. A. few moments 
of suspense, and he returned bear- 
ing the intelligence that the house 
was filled with a demented crew of 
Italians, who would listen to none 
of his entreaties. This fired the 
General to action, he hastily 
planned an an attack on the ram- 
parts, and I volunteered. my servi- 
ces as his batallion. ‘Why, damn 
it,”’ says he, ‘‘I’ll make’em under- 


stand, I speak three languages my- 
self—English, American and Texas 
—and if the two first fail Vl em- 
ploy the latter,’’ and off we started. 
Inside we found all things as re- 
ported, alien stupidity staring at 
us from all corners. ‘Who owns 
this domicile ?’’ mildly began the 
General. No answer. ‘' Who 
owns this house?’’? one octave 
higher. Continued silence. ‘‘ Where 
the h—lis'the landlord??? in high 
C. That fetched him, and my 
heart bled in sympathy as I saw 
Holland approach the fellow and 
open fire with an appeal to his 
charity. ‘‘ Would you have those 
little things dying at your’ door? 
‘Would you call down upon your- 
self the curse of Jove by your dis- 
graceful barbarity? Act like. a 
man, prove yourself the good sa- 
maritan and’’—and here, thank 
Heaven, the Italian stopped him 
by signifying his willingness to un- 
dergo anything rather than Hol- 
land. Back to camp we went in 
triumph. The darlings and their 
mamas were provided for, so 
spreading our icy blankets we 
rolled chatteringly into the arms 
of intermittant repose.

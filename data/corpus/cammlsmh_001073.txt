GEO. CC. GORHAM. 





On Friday evening, July 11th, at 
Union Hall, San Francisco, one Geo. 
C. Gorham, a disappointed Washing- 
ton office-seeking bilk, ventilated him- 
self in a strained endeavor to black- 
mail his betters and the men who gave 
George C. Gorham any prominence or 
notoriety whatever. It is the old story 
over again: “Give a nigger an inch 
and he wants a foot; give him a yard 
and he wants a rod.” The attack of 
this political fraud upon Frank Pixley 
--a man so much his superior in in- 
ic!ligence and honor that it seems like 
mockery te attempt to draw any com- 
parisen—is without any parallel, ex- 
cept amongst the lowest sort of thieves 
or stage robbers whe, occasionally, 
one is found to “peach” upon those 
who are willing-to divide in case any- 
thing is made. The most singular 
thing about the business is that the 
Chronicle and other papers, that here- 
tofore have written this barnacle upon 
the Republican party down, are now 
endeavoring to sustain him. That is, 
it is singular to those who are not 
posted as to the influence governing 
the Chronicle and papers of that ilk. 
Its success, in a financial point of view, 
has been so great, and George C. Gor- 
ham is so well aware of that fact, that 
it required but little argument on the 
part of the De Youngs and other 
Honerable Bilks to convince George 
that, since he is doomed to defeat as a 
candidate for a United States Senator 
from California, he might as well pur- 
sue a similar blackmailing course, and 
thereby add to his exchequer. We 
are not apologists for the Central 
Pacific Railroad Company. Their 
deeds may have been of the most in- 
famous character, but when aman like 
George C. Gorham, who was breught 
to political light through the coin 
and influence of these, and other what 
he pleases to denominate “monopolies,” 
then we enter our protest. His dia- 
tribes last Friday evening upon Pixley 
and the railroad company were low, 
senseless and ungrateful, and prove 
beyond a doubt that any man who has 
been elevated to the position to which 
he attained through the same instru- 
mentality which he now would ruin, 
is not fit to receive the votes of the 
people of California for any office, nor 
should the people deign to listen to his 
regular harangues. If he will go back 
upen his generous masters, will he 
prove true to the people? Assuredly 
not. He has proved himself te be a 
dishonorable bilk, and should get back 
to Washington as soqgn as possible, for 
California has no use for such a filthy 
carpet-bagger. We did not suppose 
that Kearney would ever find his coun- 
terpart in low blackguardism, false- 
hood and billingsgate, but George C. 
has entered the lists, and the sand-lot 
orator must look to his laurels. He is 
now laying his plans for a heavy bid 
from the railroad and other corpora- 
tive institutions, but if they are foolish 
enough to give him a two-bit piece we 
hope the whole honorable bilk party 
may be elected and will then take a 


course to cinch them to death.

crease.. In Illinois there is an increase of 
seven percent. The States west of the

Raum’s Circular. 


WASHINGTON, July 1s.—Commissioner 
Raum has sent circulars to all Collectors 
of Internal Revenue in the moonshine 
districts, informing them that although 
the usual appropriation for the fees of the 


United States Marshals have not been 
made by Congress, his office will not relax 
the efforts to suppress fraud bv the seizure 
of any illicit distillery and the prosecu- 
tion of offenders. He directs the Col- 
lectors to relieve as much as possible the 
Marshals from the embarrassments of 
their situation, and informs them that he 
proposes to give them the necessay force 
to execute the laws.

Injunction. 


Many inquiries having been made about 
the North Noonday and Orient contro- 
versy, we sent our reporter to inquire into 
the matter. It appears that the North 
Noonday claim was located in the year 
1875—1500 feet long by 600 feet wide— 
upon a ledge discovered in ashafton the 
line between the Noonday and North 
Noonday claims, the Noonday elaiming in 
& southerly direction and the North Noon- 
day in a northerly direction. In the case 
of the Noonday Company against the 
Live Yank, tried at the last term of the 
District Court, it was decided, upon full 
investigation, that at the time of the loca- 
tion of these claims both the United 
States law and the lecal law of Bodie 
Mining District authorized the location of 
claims 1500 feet in length by 600 feet in 
width, and conferred upon the parties all 
veins within such surface boundaries. The 
North Noonday Company, after its first 
location and while in possession, discoy- 
ered the Key Stone and Kast Noonday 
veins within the limits of the first loca- 
tion, and located them as a matter of pre- 
caution. The Noonday and North Noon- 
day companies have been for along time 
sinking a joint shaft,and have hoisting 
works inferior to none in the State, and 
are jointly building a first-class thirty- 
stamp mill. The Orient claim was a loca- 
tion to the west of the North Noonday. A 
shaft was.sunk on this claim to the depth 
ofa hundred feet, and a drift run west 
from the bottom of the shaft two hundred 
and sixty feet into and nnder the surface 
of the North Noonday claim. In this 
drift a very important strike was reported 
some weeks ago; but everything) moved 
on quietly until within a few days, when 
the Orieat managers went upon the sur- 
face of the Noonday claim, near ‘the lode 
line thereof, and a short distance north of 


| the Noonday hoisting works, and com- 


menced to pnt up a donkey engine and 
sink a shaft. It was reported that the 
Noonday managers would resist this en- 
creachment by force, and a collision was 
feared; but they deny that they ever had 
such intention, and say that they rely upon 


the law for protection; that violence has 
already ruined too many mining camps. 
We commend the course adopted—to let 
the Courts decide. The practice which is 
said to have been adopted. by the Orient 
Company, of running under ground into 
their neighbor’s claim, cannot strengthen 
their case or injure the title of the North 
Noonday company when the facts shall 
have been fully developed, and the prompt 
appeal to a Court of Justice upon the dis- 
covery of the true situation is creditable 
and proper. We do not desire to prejudge 
any case, but when parties come among 
us and purchase the oldest and best known 
titles of prospectors in possession, and 
give employment to large numbers of 
men, they are entitled to the protection of 
the law, and we hope that justice may be 
done in the present case;: .

of milk, which jit drinks from a spoon.

horned-frog in this country, the little 
reptile is a lizard. Naturalists callit an 
ignauian lizard of the genus phrynosoma.

as close observers ef every living thing 
met with in the wilds as any naturalist,

when the female is teased by a dog it 
ejects twosmall streams or slender threads 
ef blood—at least a red liquid resembling 
bleod. The liquid is spurted to the dis-

fense against foxes, wolves and such ani- 
mals, and whatever may be _ its nature it 
renders a dog very uncomfortable in the 
region of the stomach. One dose of it 
satisfies his curiosity.

County Seat Business. — Strenuous 
efforts will be made in proper time for the 
removal of the county seat to Bodie. Bodie 
being now a fixture, it is not questioned 
but there will be a Bodie as long as there 
is a county called Mono. New county 
buildings are required, and the question 
of location we think can be very easily 
settled. The less that can be said just at 
present by the press is, we opine, better 


for the cause. It is like administering an 
overdose to a candidate for public posi- 
tion. It is detrimental rather than bene- 
ficial to him. Quiet, hard workers are 
now in the field, and to them beleng the 
laurels. Let not the slightest obstacle be 
threwn in their way.

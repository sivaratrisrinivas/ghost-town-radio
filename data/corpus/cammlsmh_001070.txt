CHINESE POLICY. 





The following is said to be the 
policy of the Chinese government 
now being pursued in their inter- 
course with United States, as laid 
down by Broadstreet’s Commercial 
Directory, which says: 


The Chinese government has sur- 
prised California by revealing a policy 
quite unexpected. It has inaugurated 


. an official retrograde emigration of Ce 


lestials back to China! The last out- 
going steamer was engaged to take 
back its full complement of working 
Chinamen for government, industries, 
with orders to the consulate to confinuc 
selecting skilled hands best versed in 
our mechanical arts, etc. The policy 
appears to be to continue sending raw 
“natives, but not materially to increase 
the number of our Mongolian popula 
tion, skilled Chinamen being taken 
away in proportion. This is admirable 
strategy. China undoubtedly means 
business, and has determined to enter 
into general manufacturing, to employ 
her people and to compete with other 
nations for the trade of the Orient. We 
have sufficiently trained a few hundred 
thousand of her people in all sorts, of 
occupations, and given them skilFin 
the use of modern machinery; and 
they prove remarkably skillfal in.ewv 
erything they undertake. With, this 
nucleus for a start, Chinese industry, 
economy and perseverange, may soon 
make their rivalry felt. 


We see nothing, in, this to be sur- 
prised at, as, their intelligence and 
industry: has always been acknow!l- 
edged, and their ability to not only 
successtully compete with usin any 
of the industries in which they en- 
gage, but to drive us from the field 
has been practically demonstrated 
upon a large scale in San Fran- 
cisco, and to a less extent 
throughout the State. This be- 
ing the case with the China- 
men now among us, taken from the 
lowest order of theix society, it is 
reasonable, to,suppose that the high 
dignitartes of that empire are far 
superior in all the attributes of in- 
telligence and mental power, and 
are possessed of a full knowledge 
of the science of government. The 
latter gained within the last twen- 
ty years in their forced contact 
with the outside world. 

In all matters of diplomacy they 
have proven themselves at least 
the equal of the great powers of the 
earth, and it is questionable in our 
mind whether they do not event- 
ually drive every foreign flag from 
the Chinese seas and absorb all 
the ‘carrying trade with foreign 
nations, and successfully drive the 
products of other nations from the 
market. That they can manufac- 
ture cotton goods, silks, cloths, and 
in fact'eyery article of commerce— 
having the machinery and skilled 
labor at hand—cheaper than any 
other nation, no Californian will 
deny. and being so, it follows as 
only a question of time before this 
state of facts will actually exist. 

France, Germany and England 
will be the great sufferers in the 
operations of this policy, as with 
them it will be a race for life, and 
it is more than possible that seeing 
the danger they may avert it by 
force.

- NEARING A JUNCTION, 





From our New Mexican ex- 
changes. we learn that the Atchi- 
son, Topeka & Santa Fe road has 
rails laid 40 miles south of Fort 
Craig, and about 16 miles north of 
where the road will turn to cross 
the Rio Grande, on its way to the 
Florida mountains, where it will 
meet the Southern Pacific. From 
Martin’s Well, south of, and on 
the west side of the river, grading is 
being done. Itis the intention to 
meet the other road by January 1, 
1881. From San Marcial, which is 
the last station on the road at 
the present writing, to Martin’s 
Well, 39 miles, the rails are 
laid, but passengers and freight are 
left at the former place. It is 
claimed by the management that. 
this road will be completed to the 
Florida mountains, south of Fort 
Jummings, by the first day of the 
coming year. It appears to us to 
be an Herculean effort; buta compa- 
ny that has accomplished so much 
may be able to do even that. 

The Southern Pacific will be at a 
point on the Rio Grande 65 miles 
east of the Floridas, by the time the 
A. T. & S. F. line reaches the Flor- 
idas. The 8, P. people are building 
the road at the rate of three miles 
per day, and will certainly be at El 
Paso by February 1, 1881. The 
main line of the Atchinson road is 


also aiming for El Paso and the 
City of Mexico; and between these 
two points they are said to have 
6,000 men at work. The Texas Pa- 


cific are pushing their line along to. 


El Paso. This company has pur- 
chased old Fort Bliss, one mile and 
a half below El Paso, for a station, 
and paid $10,000 for it.

A Whole Newspaper Telegraphed | 


in fiai? avi Hour, 


We are enabled to lay before the readers 
of the Post this morning a piece of exelus- 
ive intelligence which will be found of in- 
tense and universal interest. It reaches 


us by the last mail from London, and indi-. 


cates that the Old and New Worlds are 
about to hail the approaching dawn of 
cheap telegraphy on land and under sea 
between und throughout all the corntries 
of the civilized globe, but more particularly 
across the Atlantic Ocean, between Europe 
and Amegica. Welearn that the American 
Cable Company of New York through its 
#eneral Agent, who is now in London has 
justiclosed, signed and sealed one of the 
ost important contracts that was ever 
connected with the subject of telegraphing, 
and by which the new company has secured 
the exclusive right to the use of a new in- 
vention, or process for sending messages 
by cables and otherwise. One party to the 
contract guarantees to send ten megtages 
of twenty words each, per minute through 
the connecting wire between Paris or Lon- 
don and New York. ‘This is the minimum 
speed stated in the contract; but it is said 
that at the maximum speed what could be 
sent in thirty minutes would be equal to 
the number of words contained in one num- 
ber of the London Z'imes or the New York 
Herald, And it is also asserted that by 
this process an entire number of either of 
the above-named journals eould be dis- 
patched through the cabies from New York 
to London or Paris, or from London to New 
York, aud be reproduced at those points in 
fac simile, on a stereotyped block or plate, 
complete and ready to be printed from, in 
thirty minutes, and at a trifling expense. 
This would make an average rate of speed 
of about 2,000 words per minute—Doston 
Post.
